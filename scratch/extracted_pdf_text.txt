LogicLot Landing Page — Change Requests 
Prepared for developer handoff · logiclot.ai V1 preview review · June 20, 2026 
This document lists every change needed on the current landing page preview before it's approved. Items are
grouped by priority. The six items marked   CONTENT ERROR   are not cosmetic — they describe product
capabilities and integrations that do not exist and need to come down regardless of anything else in this list.

1   Content Errors — Fix First 
1.1. Wrong auction listed as fully supported — IAA is missing and
misspelled 
CONTENT ERROR 
The “Parse run lists from all major auctions” section lists   Manheim, ADESA, CarMax, and “Americas Auto
Auction”   as the four fully-supported auctions, each with its own logo card and a “FULLY SUPPORTED” badge.
LogicLot's actual V1 auction support is   Manheim, ADESA (Openlane), CarMax Auctions, and IAA . “Americas
Auto Auction” is not a V1-supported auction and should not appear as a primary card.
IAA is currently relegated to the small “+ More supported” footnote at the bottom of the card list, and is misspelled
there (see 1.2 for every instance and the correct spelling). 
Fix:   Replace the “Americas Auto Auction” card with an   IAA   card, give it the same “FULLY SUPPORTED” /
“AUTO-PARSE” badge treatment as Manheim, ADESA, and CarMax, and remove Americas Auto Auction from the
primary four entirely (it can move to the “more supported” footnote if anywhere). 
1.2. “IAA” is misspelled in multiple places sitewide   CONTENT ERROR 
The correct auction name is   IAA   (Insurance Auto Auctions). The current build spells it incorrectly as “IAAI” in
several places — IAAI is their website domain (iaai.com), not the company name. Find every instance across the
site and correct it to   IAA   — this includes the “+ More supported” footnote on the auctions section, the “IAAI &
Auction Parsing” feature card (in the section being deleted per 2.1, but check for the same text reused elsewhere),
and the FAQ / “Upload your run lists” step description (“Drop PDF or CSV wholesale run lists from Manheim,
ADESA, CarMax, or IAAI”).
Do a sitewide find for the auction name and confirm every instance is spelled   IAA   — not IAAI or any other variant. 
1.3. “Max Bid Calculator” section uses a formula that does not match the
product 
CONTENT ERROR 
The “Meet your secret weapon, LogicLot Calculator” section shows a “Profit Equation Breakdown” that computes
Max Bid as: Est. Retail Comp Average   −   Dealer Profit Margin Reserve (15%)   −   Est. Segment Reconditioning   −   Est.
Auction Buyer Fee = Recommended Max Bid.
This formula does not exist anywhere in LogicLot's actual spec. The real adjustment logic is the history-report flag
system (Salvage   − 30%, Rebuilt   − 25%, Major Accident   − 10%, Lemon Buyback   − 15%, TMU   − 25%, stacked and
capped at   − 60%) which adjusts retail value, which then flows into max bid — there is no “15% margin reserve”, 
--- Page 2 ---
“segment reconditioning”, or “auction buyer fee” constant in the spec.
The vehicle list in this section (“Luxury Midsize SUV,” “Premium Hybrid Sedan,” “3.2 CR” condition-report
shorthand) is also generic placeholder content, not representative of how real run list data (VIN, make/model/trim,
mileage, CR score) appears elsewhere on the product. 
Decision needed from us, not just the dev:   either remove this section for V1, or rebuild it to accurately reflect the
real history-flag adjustment logic. Flagging this rather than just deleting it since it could be a useful section if it's
accurate — we should decide together before the dev rebuilds it. 
1.4. “Customize everything with integrations” section lists integrations that
don't exist 
CONTENT ERROR 
This section claims live integrations with   vAuto DMS Sync, DealerSocket CRM, Carfax Valuation, QuickBooks,
and Supabase DB , plus a claim of “Explore 21,000+ API connections.”
None of these integrations exist in the current build or spec. Supabase is an internal infrastructure choice (if used
at all), not a customer-facing integration. Carfax/AutoCheck integration is explicitly listed as out-of-scope for V1 in
the product spec.
This is a false advertising risk if it goes live as-is — delete this entire section (see 2.2). 
1.5. “AI co-pilot” section references MMR, not our actual data source   CONTENT ERROR 
The “Your dealership has entered the AI age” section's “Universal Parser Live Output” panel includes a line item
labeled   “MMR valuation mapping”   with a “Comp matches found” status.
LogicLot's actual retail value / comp data source is   MarketCheck   (via
/v2/predict/car/us/marketcheck_price/comparables), not MMR. MMR is a Manheim-specific valuation product we
don't use or integrate with. Replace “MMR valuation mapping” with something accurate, e.g. “Retail comp
matching” or “MarketCheck valuation lookup.”
The other two line items in this panel (“VIN scan OCR logic,” “Fallback tier applied”) are directionally fine and don't
need to change, just the MMR reference. 
1.6. “Source more in more places” section references a “Bidding
Companion app” that doesn't exist 
CONTENT ERROR 
The “Bid face to face” feature card under “Source more in more places” says: “Bid in person and keep online and
in-lane target bounds in sync with the Bidding Companion app.” There is no mobile/companion app in our product

3   UI / Style Changes 
3.1. Top navigation bar should match Shopify's floating style — not a pill
container 
UI / STYLE 
Reference: shopify.com navigation. On Shopify, the nav links (“Why Shopify,” “Products,” “Pricing,” “Enterprise”) sit
directly on the dark background with no rounded pill or container behind them. Only the action items on the right
(“Log in” text link and “Start for free” button) are distinct elements, and the button is a plain white pill — no extra
outer pill wrapping the whole nav.
LogicLot's current nav wraps the entire link group in a large rounded pill/oval, and individual items look boxed-in
rather than floating. Remove the outer container so the logo and nav links float freely against the dark background,
matching Shopify's treatment, and keep only the CTA button as a standalone pill. 
3.2. Recommendation: tone down the AI-buzzword visual treatment for a dealer
audience 
UI / STYLE 
Our target buyer (Used Car Director, Pre-Owned Manager, Dealer Principal) is not a SaaS/tech buyer. The current
page's reliance on a Shopify-style “tech startup” aesthetic (neon purple glow, “AI co-pilot,” “intelligent companion,”
“edge network,” “serverless runners”) reads more like an AI/dev-tools product than a tool built for car dealers.
Recommend keeping the dark theme (it photographs well and feels premium) but replacing jargon-heavy section
titles and copy with plain, dealer-recognizable language throughout — see Section 6 for specific copy fixes. This is
a broader pass the dev should apply to any remaining section not explicitly listed below. 
3.3. Simplify the top nav to four items: Home, Why LogicLot, Pricing, Contact   UI / STYLE 
The current nav has extra items (e.g. “Products” dropdown, “Sign In”). Reduce the nav to exactly four links:   Home,
Why LogicLot, Pricing, Contact , plus the single CTA button (see 4.1 for its new label). Remove “Sign In” and any
“Products” dropdown from the top nav. 
3.4. “Contact” should open a contact form pop-up, not navigate to a page   UI / STYLE 
Clicking “Contact” in the nav should open a pop-up / modal directly on the page, not route to a separate contact
page.
The pop-up should contain four fields:   Full Name, Email Address, Phone Number, and Message , plus a submit
button. Dev's choice on backend (e.g. send to info@logiclot.ai, or pipe into HubSpot as a new contact/activity) —
flag whichever approach is simplest for V1 and we'll confirm.

5   Pricing Page Rebuild 
5.1. Replace the Pricing page with our finalized pricing reference   UI / STYLE 
We already sent the dev a finalized pricing reference design (LogicLot_Pricing_Final_v2.pdf) showing three tier
cards above a full feature-comparison table. The new Pricing page should follow that layout: a card per tier (price,
billing cadence, upload allowance, one short value line), followed by a feature-by-feature comparison table with

Important — before the dev builds this, we need to confirm final tier numbers ourselves.   The reference PDF
shows SMB $199/mo (2 uploads/day), Mid $399/mo (6 uploads/day), and Enterprise as “Contact Sales” with
unlimited uploads. Our actual finalized pricing is   Independent $199/mo (2 uploads/day), Midsize $399/mo (5

— a flat published price, not “Contact Sales.” Reconcile the reference PDF's numbers against this before handing it
to the dev so we don't ship two different sets of pricing.
